Categories & Concepts 🧠

How a finite mind makes sense of an infinite world — a visual study platform prepared for Sezen.

A note from your professor

Dear Sezen — every second you are awake, your mind is quietly sorting. You walk into a room you have never seen and instantly know: that is a chair, that is a window, that furry thing is a cat. You never reason your way there. This module is about how that happens.

Why it matters: concepts are the hidden scaffolding of intelligent behaviour. Without them every object would be brand new and nothing you learned yesterday would help you today. And — as you know from your DEI work — the same machinery that recognises chairs also shapes how we think about groups of people.

How to read this: read the Key Terms below first. Then open the lessons in order — each one is built around a single diagram. Study the picture first, then the bullets, then say the memory hook out loud. Answer each Comprehension Check before opening the answer. Finish with the flashcards and the MCQ exam. — Your Professor

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Explain the difference between a category (in the world) and a concept (in the mind), and why concepts are central to intelligent behaviour.
  • Understand the problems with defining categories — necessary and jointly sufficient features.
  • Understand fuzzy boundaries, borderline members and typicality (Hampton; McCloskey & Glucksberg; Rosch).
  • Explain family resemblance theory as the source of typicality (Rosch & Mervis, 1975).
  • Describe category hierarchies and the basic-level advantage — and why it shifts with culture and expertise.
  • Compare prototype and exemplar theories of concept representation, and the modern multiple-systems view.
  • Explain how knowledge influences concept learning, and what psychological essentialism is (Keil, 1989) — including its social risks.

📚 Key Terms — read these first

TermPlain-language definitionExample
CategoryA set of things in the world that can be treated as equivalent in some way.all trucks
ConceptThe mental representation of a category — your mental “file”.what you know about trucks
Well-defined (classical) categoryA category with a definition that says exactly what is in and out.“a dog is a four-legged animal that barks”
Necessary featuresFeatures every member must have.all dogs breathe
Jointly sufficient featuresFeatures that together guarantee membership.has all of X, Y, Z → member
Fuzzy categoryA category whose boundary is unclear and can shift over time.vegetable (seaweed? tomato?)
Borderline memberNot clearly in, not clearly out.sink as a kitchen utensil
TypicalityHow “good” an example a member is — from most typical to borderline.robin > penguin
AtypicalA poor (but real) member.ostrich
PrototypeThe most typical member / the summary of the category.robin for “bird”
Family resemblanceMembers share overlapping features; typical items have common in-category features and few features of other categories.robin vs penguin
Category hierarchyCategories nested inside bigger ones.brown bear → bear → mammal
SuperordinateThe general, abstract level.animal, furniture
Basic levelThe neutral, preferred, intermediate level.dog, chair
SubordinateThe specific level.terrier, desk chair
DifferentiationMembers similar to each other and different from other categories.chairs
Prototype theoryA concept is one summary description: a list of weighted features.“birds: wings ★★★, eat worms ★”
Weighted featuresFeatures weighted by how frequent they are in the category.feathers = high weight
ExemplarA remembered example labelled as a category member.your family dog
Exemplar theoryA concept is the collection of remembered examples; no summary.all the vegetables you’ve seen
Knowledge approachConcepts are learned and used in light of what we know about the world.donker vs blegdav
Psychological essentialismBelief that members share a hidden essence that makes them what they are and causes their features.“a raccoon is always a raccoon”
Natural kind vs artifactCategories from nature (animals, plants, minerals) vs human-made objects.raccoon vs coffeepot
